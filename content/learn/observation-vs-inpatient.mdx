## Observation status

Observation is an **outpatient** service used when the physician needs more time to evaluate whether the patient should be admitted or discharged. Patients may stay overnight and still remain observation.

Common examples: chest pain rule-out, mild dehydration, dizziness, shortness of breath pending workup.

## Inpatient status

Inpatient means the physician formally admits the patient because the condition needs a higher level of ongoing hospital care (for example sepsis, stroke, major surgery, significant pneumonia).

## Two-Midnight Rule (Medicare guideline)

As a **guideline** under Medicare:

- Expected hospital care spanning **less than two midnights** → usually observation
- Expected care spanning **two midnights or more** → generally inpatient admission

**Medical necessity always comes first.** A patient can remain observation beyond two midnights in some situations, and can be admitted sooner when the condition clearly requires inpatient care.

## Why AR cares

Wrong status drives wrong claim type, wrong benefits (Part A vs Part B), patient liability surprises, and denials. When researching hospital balances, confirm **status**, **admit/discharge dates**, and whether the remit matches outpatient or inpatient methodology.
