## The cycle at a glance

1. **Patient access** — scheduling, registration, demographics
2. **Insurance verification** — eligibility, benefits, authorizations
3. **Clinical documentation** — encounter notes that support coding
4. **Coding** — CPT, HCPCS, ICD-10-CM (and ICD-10-PCS for inpatient facility)
5. **Charge entry / claim creation** — charges become a claim
6. **Claim scrubbing** — edits catch missing data before submission
7. **Clearinghouse** — routes the claim; may reject for format/data issues
8. **Payer adjudication** — payer accepts, pays, denies, or pend
9. **Remittance** — EOB (paper) or ERA (electronic)
10. **Payment posting** — payments, adjustments, and denials hit the ledger
11. **AR follow-up** — unpaid or unclear balances are worked
12. **Denial management / appeals** — rework or formal appeal when needed
13. **Patient collections** — remaining patient responsibility after insurance

## Where AR owns the outcome

AR becomes active when:

- No remit arrives after expected turnaround
- A denial needs investigation
- Payment looks incorrect (underpay, overpay, wrong patient)
- Coordination of benefits (COB) is unresolved
- The claim is stuck in a payer pend status

## Rejections vs denials

- **Rejection** — claim never entered payer processing (clearinghouse or payer front-end edit). Fix data and resubmit as a new/corrected claim per process.
- **Denial** — payer processed the claim and refused payment (or paid $0) with a reason code. Action depends on the reason: correct and resubmit, appeal, bill secondary, or transfer to patient.

## Tip for new analysts

When you open an account, ask: **Which RCM step failed?** Eligibility? Auth? Coding? Enrollment? Filing limit? That question keeps your research focused.
