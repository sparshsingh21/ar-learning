## Why AR exists

Providers deliver care first and get paid later. **Accounts Receivable (AR)** is the work of tracking unpaid claim balances, finding out why they are unpaid, and taking the next action that moves them toward payment or a clean write-off.

AR sits near the **end** of Revenue Cycle Management (RCM). Front-end teams gather demographics and eligibility. Coding and billing create and submit claims. Payment posting records what payers paid or denied. AR steps in when money is still outstanding or the remittance does not make sense.

## What AR analysts actually do

- Review aged claim inventories (worklists / aging buckets)
- Check payer portals, clearinghouses, and EOBs/ERAs
- Call payers with targeted questions when portals are not enough
- Document analysis, research, and action in account notes
- Route coding, credentialing, or patient-balance issues to the right team
- Protect timely filing by prioritizing claims that are close to deadlines

## AR is not the same as billing or coding

| Role | Primary focus |
| --- | --- |
| Billing | Clean claim creation and submission |
| Coding | Accurate CPT / ICD / modifiers |
| Payment posting | Recording remits and adjustments |
| AR | Resolving unpaid or unclear balances after submission |

## Success signals

Teams usually watch **days in AR**, **aging over 90 days**, **denial rate**, and **first-pass resolution**. Your job as an analyst is not only to “make calls” — it is to leave every account clearer than you found it, with a documented next step.
